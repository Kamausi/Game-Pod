                    Weather market vs NWS forecastNot run yet.
                    
      
                    
        
        
        
        
      
                    
      
                    
      
      
                    BucketNWSEnsembleUsedBidAskFeeEdge¼ Kelly of $1,000
                    
      
                    Ensemble: the 82 runs of GFS (31) and ECMWF (51) from Open-Meteo's free API, each run's daily high dressed with a 1.5°F normal kernel (Roulston & Smith 2003); the spread of the runs is the forecast's real uncertainty, where σ below is only an assumption. The command-line scanner learns each station's bias and kernel, and scores every settled bucket (tradebot weather calibration). The forecast is the NWS hourly forecast at the market's own resolution station (NYC settles on LaGuardia, not Central Park), maximum over the station's local day. Buckets are whole degrees. Edge = model probability − ask − taker fee (0.05 × p × (1 − p)). σ = 3.5°F is a starting assumption: when the model's "edges" sit in the tails, that says σ is wider than the market's, not that money is on the table. The command-line scanner learns each station's own forecast bias and σ after 30 settled days (tradebot weather scan / settle).

                    EstimatorPer barAnnualisedNote
                    
        
                    Range estimators use the high and low, so they need far fewer bars than close-to-close for the same accuracy. Yang–Zhang is unbiased with gaps and drift; the desk sizes stops and estimates impact with it.
                    
        
                    Trend or noise? · Lo–MacKinlay variance ratio
                    
        
                    HorizonVRz (robust)Reading
                    
        
                    VR above 1 = moves persist (trend rules have something to work with); below 1 = moves reverse. Only a p under 0.05 counts.
                    
      
                    
      
                    
        
                    Is the edge real? · deflated Sharpe
                    
        
                    
          
          
          
        
                    
        
        
                    
        
                    Try 100 random strategies and the best one will show a Sharpe near 2.5 by luck alone (Bailey & López de Prado). The deflated Sharpe asks whether this one beats that benchmark after skew and fat tails. The insider backtest counts its own configurations and refuses to call an edge under 95%.
                    
      
                    
      
                    
        
                    Are the p_win calls honest? · calibration
                    
        
                    
        
                    Stated p_winCallsAverage statedActually won
                    
        
                    An honest forecaster's "60%" wins about 60% of the time. Rows more than 10 points off are marked. Brier below the "always say the base rate" score means the calls carry information.
                    
      
                    
      
                    
        
                    What will the order cost? · square-root impact
                    
        
                    
          
          
          
          
        
                    
        
                    
        
                    Cost ≈ half the spread + σ × √(order ÷ daily volume) (Almgren et al. 2005; Tóth et al. 2011). Every desk ticket now carries this number, and the risk check refuses tickets above 25 bps.

                    Fee schedule
                    
      
                    
      
                    Fees are a percentage of each trade, so the price of one coin does not matter: $100 of BTC and $100 of DOGE pay the same fee. The paper engine fills like a market order, so crypto pays the taker rate. US stocks at Alpaca have no commission (an SEC fee of about 0.003% applies to sells). An agent only enters when its target beats the round-trip cost, so at a 0.50% taker fee the one-minute crypto agents mostly sit out. That is the fee doing its job, not a bug.
                    
    
                    
    
                    
      
                    Account-wide limits
                    
      
                    
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
      
                    
      
                    Per-asset tiers are fixed in code. Crypto majors (BTC, ETH, SOL): up to 25% of equity, market orders, spread under 25 bps, $5M+ daily volume. Meme coins (DOGE, PEPE): up to 5%, limit orders only, spread under 50 bps, $1M+ volume. Stocks (AAPL, SPY): up to 20%, spread under 20 bps, $20M+ volume, NYSE hours only. Penny stocks under $5 (SNDL): up to 2%, limit orders only, spread under 150 bps, $500k+ volume. FX majors (EUR, GBP, AUD vs USD): up to 30%, spread under 3 bps, Sunday 17:00 to Friday 17:00 New York. Orders above 10% of equity are split into 3 TWAP slices. Exits: a fixed stop and a target twice as far away are attached at entry (majors 0.4%/0.8%, meme 1%/2%, stocks 0.3%/0.6%, penny 1.5%/3%, FX 0.08%/0.16%) and the model cannot close early unless you allow it. Size shrinks as short-term volatility rises above its norm. Every entry passes a pre-trade reviewer that blocks the riskiest 10%.
                    
    
                    
    
                    Live mode reads real prices through your Crypto.com connector (and, when switched on, stocks and FX from Yahoo Finance through Firecrawl) and trades paper money only; nothing is sent to an exchange from this page. Real-money trading runs from the command-line app after the launch gates in LAUNCH.md. The simulated market is a correlated random walk with regime changes and no edge to find, useful for watching the machinery at speed.
