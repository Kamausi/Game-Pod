Field Guide

Read the mud before you drop in

Press Z (D-pad right menu → Scout) to read the section ahead: water depth, how deep the soft stuff goes, the soil, and whether there's a firm bottom your tires can reach. The HUD names the soil under you.

  • Clay gumbo — sticky, packs the tread solid, and turns to grease the more it's churned. Wheel speed cleans the lugs.
  • Silt — weak and slick once it's worked; fine soup with no bite.
  • Wet sand — strong under load but sucks power; momentum and flotation, not wheel speed.
  • Peat muck — almost no bearing strength. Float or sink: air down and keep moving.
  • Loam — the honest stuff. Forgiving traction.

Momentum, not panic

Carry steady speed in, keep the wheel straight, and stay in it. When forward progress stops, stop spinning: every second of wheelspin digs you down onto the frame. Rock it — ease forward, reverse into your own tracks, forward again.

4WD, low range and lockers

G / LB cycles the transfer case: 2H → 4H → 4L (4L only when nearly stopped). Part-time 4WD has no centre diff: tight turns in 4H on grippy ground wind up the driveline until the front U-joint snaps. 4L multiplies torque (the garage shows your crawl ratio) — go slow and steady.

L / B engages selectable lockers. Lock before the hole — the game refuses at speed or mid-spin, like a real air locker. A locked front axle pushes straight. A spool is always locked. Open diffs send power to the wheel with the least grip; LSDs help a little; brake traction control brakes the spinning wheel.

Tires and air

Airing down (V) lengthens the footprint and drops ground pressure — you float instead of cutting in. Below about 8 psi a hard side load will roll the bead off the rim unless you run beadlocks. Big tires raise your diff clearance but make the gearing taller: the garage spec sheet gives the re-gear that restores it.

Water

Know where your air intake is. Suck water and the engine hydrolocks: it's dead until you get out of the water and let it drain, then throttle to restart. Crank it while it's still swamped and you bend a rod. A snorkel raises the intake. After a crossing your brakes are wet — drag them lightly to dry them.

Recovery: least force first

  1. Stop spinning and look (Scout).
  2. Shovel (K): dig ramps in front of the tires and clear the belly. 5 seconds.
  3. Traction boards (B): wedged under the tires that drive — ease on gently, don't spin on them or you'll chew them up. Drive clear and they're picked up.
  4. Winch (J): hooks the nearest tree, abandoned rig or post ahead within 38 m. Pull drops ~9% per layer on the drum, the motor needs rest between pulls, and shock-loading the line by driving against it snaps it. Help it with light throttle only.
  5. Dozer (T): last resort. In a judged Mud Bog pull it's the only help allowed, and it ends your run.

UTVs

The CVT belt cooks when a side-by-side slogs through mud in high range. Use low range in the slop; a burnt belt slips and won't pull.

After the run

Caked mud inside the wheels throws them out of balance (you'll feel the shake at speed). Pressure-wash & repair in the garage cleans it off and fixes anything you broke.
